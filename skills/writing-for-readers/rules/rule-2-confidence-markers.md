# Rule 2. Confidence markers on every load-bearing claim

One of the three rules the [writing taxonomy](../taxonomy.md) absorbed. The first line of the body names the cells it governs.

Governs C2, C3, C4, C5, C6, C7, and C8 where the reader will repeat the claim outward. Three levels, never more:

- **Verified**: tested directly during this work. Name the evidence; with more than one test, carry an evidence log giving request, response and reading.
- **Documented**: asserted by official vendor documentation, not tested here.
- **Inferred**: reasoned from observed behaviour, not confirmed. Say it may be wrong, and say what the reader should do if it is.

A document mixing tested steps with plausible guesses reads as uniformly authoritative, because nothing on the page separates them. The reader spends real effort on the guessed step, and when it fails they discount the whole document, including the parts that were solid. The marker sits on the step heading or beside the claim, never in a footnote. **An unmarked claim reads as Verified, so mark every load-bearing claim or none.** Prefer converting Inferred to Verified by running the test; ship an inference only where the check needs access you do not have. Carry a `Ruled out` section, itself marked. Put a compact key defining the three levels below the boundary line. In client-facing cells (C9 to C13) the markers are consumed in review and expressed by heading rather than shown as badges.
