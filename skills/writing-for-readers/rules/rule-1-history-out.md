# Rule 1. History out of current-state documents

One of the three rules the [writing taxonomy](../taxonomy.md) absorbed. The first line of the body names the cells it governs.

Governs C1, C2, C3, C4, C5. Ask of every paragraph that narrates what happened: **is this history relevant to performing the task effectively?** If not, it moves. If so, it stays as a lesson in the present tense.

The tell is a sentence that needs "there used to be", "before it", "the old value was", "first run found" or a date to make sense. It is doing two jobs, saying what is true now and justifying it with what changed, and a reader who only wants the first has to read past the second.

History that earns its place: a trap that will recur, stated as the trap ("X silently does Y", not "on a date X did Y"); a tried-and-rejected approach a future reader would plausibly try again, in one line; a measurement that still characterises current behaviour; the date a fact was last verified, as a one-line stamp.

History that moves: incident timelines, log ids, who said what when, deploy dates, "applied to X on date" notes, superseded values. A memory's history goes to the project's own notes; a README's goes to a `HISTORY.md` beside it, listed in the README and pointed at near the top. A `HISTORY.md` has dated headings, oldest first, is append-only, is never edited to mark an entry superseded (a new entry says so), and is never read for current state.
